## Localization Methods

You can go in two basic directions when localizing an app. Deep
localization involves releasing versions of the app tailored to a few
select languages and cultures. You typically release the app in local
markets and promote it via culturally-appropriate marketing campaigns.

Minimum Viable Localization (MVL) is more helpful if you don’t know which
markets your app will be most successful in. By testing it in several
different locations, you can determine the most profitable approach.

## The Process

Localizing an app involves focusing on these key factors:

* **Content**: Begin localizing an app’s content by adjusting its listing
accordingly. If this attracts new users, adjust the basic content, such as
a user manual. If you’re still successful after doing so, localize all
content, including details such as currency, graphics, and more.
* **Translation**: Your budget will determine how you may go about translating
your app to a new language. It may be more expensive to hire a
professional translation firm, but the investment is worth your money.
A professionally-translated app will provide foreign users with a better
overall experience. If you can’t afford to hire professionals, be sure to
provide your in-house translators with all the resources they need to
avoid errors.
* **Internationalization**: Sometimes you must adjust the code of the app to
successfully translate it. For example, some languages use characters not
used in English. You may need to change the code to display these
characters.
* **Testing & Optimization**: You tested your original app before releasing it
to ensure it performed reliably. Test the new versions with the same
rigor. It’s also important to edit app store listings according to what
you know about your various target markets. Once you’ve taken these steps,
launch your app and monitor its performance.

## Pros (& Cons) of Localization

It’s easy to understand why you would choose to localize an app. Doing so
helps you cast a wider net when you launch it. Even if your app’s
popularity in foreign markets doesn’t skyrocket, you can still earn more
downloads by releasing it in more countries.

You can also maximize your return-on-investment by localizing it for
several audiences. Every time you repeat this process, you learn ways to
simplify it and boost your overall efficiency.

Of course, there are some disadvantages to this process you should be
aware of. One is simply the amount of time it can take. Experts state
there are [approximately 6,909 languages](https://www.linguisticsociety.org/content/how-many-languages-are-there-world) in the world. Determining which
to translate your app into requires effort. 

Additionally, because there is no standardized development process for
localizing an app, you must take the time to find experienced people to deliver a proper translation.

That could be worthwhile. The app marketplace is crowded. You could boost
your own odds of success by reaching out to foreign markets. After all,
chances are good many non-English speakers could still benefit from using
your app. Give them that opportunity and you might just discover a new
user base.
